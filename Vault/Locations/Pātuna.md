# Pātuna

> The free [[Tunoth|eelfolk]] city where the [[Book Plotline|book]] opens — a marsh-island fortress of escaped slaves on [[Saraïm]]'s very doorstep, under a siege that cannot quite close. Its name means **"eel-weir"**: a fortress that is also a trap.

## At a glance

- A free **[[Tunoth]] (eelfolk)** [[The Ocean World|Iyim]] city-state — poor, weak, defiant.
- Built on a **marshy island** on the **coastal fringe of [[Saraïm]]** — right on the empire's border.
- **Under Saraïm siege** — a grinding stalemate it can't lose and can't win.
- **Name:** Māori *pā tuna*, an **eel-weir** (and *pā*, a fortified village). The eelfolk's fortress *is* a great living weir in the marsh. *(The Saraïm have their own contemptuous name for it — TBD.)*

## Where it is

On the **marshy coast of Saraïm itself**, not off in the scattered Iyim — a free Kilayim holdout on the empire's own border. That placement is why the empire **can** besiege it, why it **can't ignore** it (defiance in plain sight), and why the later chase to [[Zebul]] stays geographically tight.

## History — a marsh of runaways

Pātuna was founded, and is forever refilled, by **eelfolk who slipped out of [[Saraïm]] bondage.** Being slime-slick escape artists ([[Tunoth]]), they fled into the one place the empire couldn't follow — the deadly bog only amphibious eelfolk can live in. Over generations a fugitive haven grew into a poor, proud free city: **a maroon colony of the empire's own escaped "unclean."**

## Why it holds (the stalemate)

- **The marsh makes it borderline impossible to take.** No army can march or assault across the bog; the amphibious, slime-slick eelfolk own this drowned ground.
- **It can't be starved** — they fish the surrounding waters for endless food.
- So the siege is a **grinding stalemate.**

## Why it's being sieged

Two reasons, fused:
- **Ideological affront** — free, ungrovelling "unclean" Kilayim on the empire's border is intolerable to a purity-fascist creed ([[Saraïm]]).
- **A hemorrhage** — Pātuna is the **destination every escaping eelfolk dreams of;** it bleeds the empire's labor and inspires more flight. Crush the beacon, stop the bleeding.

## How the stalemate breaks (the book)

A **slow strangulation** the **crew's arrival tips** ([[Book Plotline]]). The Saraïm blockade chokes Pātuna's trade and lifelines for years; then the crew wash in — and Mateus's confided secret, **traded by his friend, buys the siege's end at the cost of the crew.** (The city is *saved*; the crew are sold out.)
