# The Pirate

> **Mateus Corvo.** A Portuguese street-urchin turned reluctant pirate, and the **only human aboard who has already crossed** — survivor of a doomed voyage that chased the star for riches and found monsters instead.

## At a glance

- **Role:** the mission's **guide to the other side** — the one man who has **been across and lived**
- **Affiliation:** [[The Voodoo Pirates]] — but loosely; never a true believer in their voodoo
- **Origins:** a **street urchin**, came up from nothing
- **Age:** **early twenties**
- **Status:** alive
- **Belief:** a pragmatic survivor, not a cultist

## Background

- He grew up a **street urchin**, and in his **early-to-mid teens** was **recruited into a pirate crew** — he signed on thinking it would be an **adventure, something fun.**
- He quickly learned the crew was **weird — deep into voodoo** ([[The Voodoo Pirates]]). Not his thing; he went along to get by, never to believe.
- The crew kept an **astrology witch** who **half-knew, from old legends, about the star and the crossing** ([[The Crossing]]). She **overhyped** it — promised (or claimed) that following the star led to **riches.**
- This came only **a few years into his time aboard** — he was still a **teenager, maybe sixteen or seventeen.** They chased the star and **crossed.** Beyond it there was no treasure: there were **sea monsters** (the alien life of another world's waters — see [[Setting Principles]], [[The Known Worlds]]). They **turned and ran**, and **most of the crew were eaten.** Only a few survived — him among them — limping home by sheer luck, the way the voodoo pirates always have.
- That is how he "technically **has been to another world**," even if he never set foot on its shore: he crossed its seas and barely escaped them.
- In the years after, the voodoo madness behind him, he **drifted into ordinary piracy** — no more star-chasing — through his late teens and into his **early twenties.**

## How he ends up on the mission

- By his **early twenties**, running with an **ordinary pirate crew**, he made the fatal mistake of helping try to **rob the mission's ship as it prepared to set out** ([[The Pope's Mission]]).
- They were hopelessly **outmatched** by [[The Captain]]'s elite crew — realized they were **in over their heads, and surrendered.** Now he's a captured pirate, bound for the noose.
- But as a prisoner he **overhears the crew talking about the star** — the crossing, the plan. He grasps two things at once: he **knows that secret firsthand**, and he now knows **too much to simply be set free.**
- So he **uses his knowledge to survive** — revealing he's one of the only men alive to have **chased the star and lived.** That makes him too valuable to hang, and they take him aboard. His "willingness" to serve the Church is, at bottom, the choice between **the mission and the gallows.**

## Personality

- **Streetwise survivor.** Raised hard, kept alive by wits and luck; he reads people and danger fast.
- **Not a believer** — not in the pirates' voodoo, and not, much, in anything else. He went looking for adventure once and got horror; he's warier now.
- The only one aboard who **knows in his gut what the open dark can do to a crew.** The others imagine the crossing; he has survived it.

## Relationships / frictions

- [[The Captain]] — a navy captain would normally see a man like him **hanged**; instead they're the **two real sailors** aboard, forced to trust each other.
- [[The Chaplain-Knight]] — the holy zealot and the godless survivor; the knight may see a sinner to be saved.
- [[The Lutheran]] — two outsiders to Rome, oddly aligned, though they share little else.

## On the Nephilim

- **No patience for the "hooty-tooty theology."** To him they're **just mythological creatures** — monsters, not demons or fallen-angel spawn ([[Real-World Nephilim Theology]]).
- The irony: he's the **only one who's actually seen them**, yet the least interested in what they *mean.*

## Arc hook: kindness, then betrayal

A planned beat: of all the crew, the Pirate is the one who **softens** — he starts to **feel bad for the Nephilim (or their downtrodden hybrids) and shows some kindness.** And he is **betrayed for it.** The lesson lands hard against the theology: these are beings with **no redeemable soul** ([[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]) — his mercy is misplaced, and it costs him. *(In the [[Book Plotline|book]]: he confides the secret of Earth to an eelfolk friend, who trades it away to save their city.)*
