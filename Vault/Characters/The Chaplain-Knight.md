# The Chaplain-Knight

> **Don Bartolomeo della Croce.** An Italian survivor of Rhodes, a former Knight Hospitaller chaplain turned village priest, called by the Pope to **lead** the mission. The zealous, devout heart of the crew, and its most loyal man.

## At a glance

- **Role:** **leader** of the 1500s mission; its spiritual authority and its sword
- **Age:** a little over **50**
- **From:** Italy; now serves a parish **just north of Rome**
- **Faith:** **very** Catholic — a true believer, a zealot
- **Status:** alive
- **Former order:** Knights Hospitaller (Order of St. John) — as a **chaplain-knight** (a priest who also fought)

## Background

- He fought as a **chaplain-knight** of the **Knights Hospitaller** and **survived the Siege of Rhodes (1522)** — the legendary defeat that left the Order homeless.
- **He left the Order** for two reasons: it **no longer had a home** (this is the **mid-1520s**, before the Order was given Malta in 1530), and he was **getting older** — a little over fifty.
- He settled as a **local priest just north of Rome**, and his history made him **a bit of a local legend** — the old knight who lived through Rhodes.

## Why he's on the mission

- The **Pope personally called him up to lead the team.**
- He is **overjoyed** to be chosen. Two hungers had been gnawing at him in his quiet parish:
  - the **itch to defend the weak in combat** again, the thing he was made for, and
  - the need to feel he has a **purpose** once more.
- This mission gives him both. He believes, body and soul, that it is God's work.

## Personality & role on the crew

- **The zealot.** To him a Nephilim is no mystery to be studied but an enemy of God to be faced. (See the irony below.)
- **The leader.** The mission's commander and its moral authority — the others answer to him.
- **The most loyal.** More devoted to the **Pope and the mission** than anyone else aboard.
- **Insists on "Don"** (his dignity as a parish priest); [[The Lutheran|Konrad]] mockingly calls him **"Fra'"** to drag him back to his disbanded order. A running needle between them.

## Friction

- He shares a small boat with a **Lutheran heretic** ([[The Pirate|the pirate]] is a pragmatic sinner; the Lutheran is, to him, something worse — a soul in rebellion against the Church he'd die for). His zeal and the German's reform are a collision waiting to happen.
- As the Pope's man and a true believer, he is the one most likely to choose **the mission's orders over mercy or curiosity.**

## The irony (canon hook)

Per [[Why Earth Is Forbidden]], the Nephilim's deepest fear is precisely **a divinely-favored human, sent to exterminate them.** This man — zealous, devout, sent by the head of the Church — is **the Nephilim's nightmare made flesh,** and he doesn't know it yet.

## On the Nephilim

- **They must die — eventually.** The one thing he and [[The Lutheran|the heretic]] agree on (see [[Real-World Nephilim Theology]]).
- He is **decently abolitionist** on slavery — a conviction likely to bite when the crew meets the Nephilim's fear-bound, hybrid underclass ([[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]).
- Like everyone of his time he'd default to the **Sethite reading** ("corrupt men, not monsters") — until the giant corpse forces the **fallen-angel** truth on him.
