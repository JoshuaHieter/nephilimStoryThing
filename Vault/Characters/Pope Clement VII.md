# Pope Clement VII

> *(Historical.)* Giulio de' Medici, Pope **1523–1534** — the real pope of the late 1520s, and the man who convenes [[The Pope's Mission]]. A pontiff freshly **humiliated and battered** by the 1527 Sack of Rome.

## At a glance

- **Who:** Giulio de' Medici, a **Medici** pope
- **Reign:** 1523–1534
- **In our window (late 1520s):** **just survived the Sack of Rome (1527)** — besieged in Castel Sant'Angelo, ransomed, escaped in disguise. His prestige in ruins, but still the figurehead of Christendom.
- **Role:** **instigator** — convenes the mission to investigate the non-human body

## Why he calls the mission

- A non-human corpse is **theological** — the Church's business by right ([[The Pope's Mission]]).
- He is still the **nearest thing to a pan-European authority**, even battered — the one figure who can assemble a cross-national crew.
- He wants **an investigation**, nothing grander.

## Context: a weakened pope

- The **1527 Sack of Rome** left him **traumatized and dependent on Emperor Charles V** — his unpaid, partly-**Lutheran** Imperial army had stormed and gutted Rome.
- That weakness ripples outward: beholden to Charles V (uncle to Catherine of Aragon), Clement **won't grant Henry VIII's annulment**, which pushes **England** toward its own break with Rome in 1533–34 — just *after* our story.
- **Irony worth keeping:** Lutheran soldiers sacked the seat of Catholicism — and now Clement must seat a [[The Lutheran|Lutheran]] at the heart of his own holy mission.
