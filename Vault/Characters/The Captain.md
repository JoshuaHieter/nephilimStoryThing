# The Captain

> **Captain Edmund Drake.** A renowned English sea-captain in his early forties, victor of many naval battles — brought along not for his faith but because **his crew is the best afloat.**

## At a glance

- **Role:** **master of the ship** and commander of its crew — the man who actually makes the voyage happen
- **From:** England
- **Age:** **early forties**
- **Faith:** **nominally Catholic** — but he doesn't much care, beyond **appearances and custom**
- **Status:** alive
- **Brings:** an **elite crew**, plus a lifetime of **naval command, navigation, and battle**

## Why he's on the mission

- He's a **renowned captain** who has **won multiple naval engagements** — the obvious man to command the expedition's ship.
- But the real reason is his **crew: the best there is.** Reaching the star and surviving what waits beyond *took*, they believed, a top ship and top hands ([[The Pope's Mission]]) — so he brings exactly that. *(In truth a small boat would have done; they just didn't know it — [[The Crossing#You don't need a big ship]].)*
- **Religion has nothing to do with it.** In the **late 1520s**, England is still Catholic, so a nominal Catholic serving the Pope is unremarkable — but his selection is about competence, not creed.

## Personality & role

- **A worldly pragmatist.** Where the [[The Chaplain-Knight|knight]] sees a holy war and the [[The Lutheran|German]] sees scripture and language, the Captain sees a **problem to be sailed and, if need be, fought.** He's the grounded, secular head on a boat of zealots and obsessives.
- His Catholicism is **cultural and for show** — he'll cross himself and keep the forms, and feel nothing much behind them.
- He is the **competent adult in the room** — and that makes him the natural counterweight to the mission's true believers.
- **Irreverent to the bone.** When Konrad translates the god-king's title — *[[Zebul|Melek ha-Kokhavim]]*, "King of the Stars" — Edmund hears **"cock have him"** and never lets it go, calling the Melek that for the rest of the book. Levity, and pure contempt for the whole pompous empire.

## The frictions

- **Command tension (built in).** *He* commands the ship and its crew; the **Chaplain-Knight leads the mission.** When the seaman's judgment and the holy man's orders collide, who wins?
- **vs. [[The Pirate]].** It was the Captain's own crew that **beat back and captured** the Pirate's raiders when they tried to rob the ship before departure — so by rights he'd see the man **hanged**, not seated beside him. Yet they are the **only two real sailors** aboard, now forced to trust each other. Lawman and outlaw on one deck.

## The stranding beat (his personal stakes)

He brings his crew — **the best afloat, his life's pride** — across the star, trusting the [[The Crossing|reverse-engineered method]] and the Pope's command. Then the **sea monsters take them.** He lives; **most of his men are devoured.** The Captain ends up marooned carrying the heaviest stone on the boat: **he led the best crew in the world to be eaten, and survived it.** Command guilt and survivor's guilt, fused.

## On the Nephilim

- **He doesn't much care whether they live or die.** He sees them as he sees **the "savages" his motherland rules** — lessers and subjects, a fact of empire, not a theological crisis ([[Real-World Nephilim Theology]]).
- *(For a 1520s Englishman, the nearest such "subjects" are the Gaelic Irish his crown is busy subjugating — a likely frame of reference.)*
- His **colonial indifference** sets him against the **abolitionist** instincts of the knight and the Lutheran.

## Connections

- [[The Pope's Mission]] — the expedition he sails.
- [[The Chaplain-Knight]] — the mission's leader; his command rival.
- [[The Pirate]] — the outlaw he'd normally hang; his fellow seaman.
- [[The Crossing]] — the voyage, and the star his crew won't follow.

## Open questions

- How does a proud commander **live with** leading his beloved crew to slaughter — does it break him, harden him, or drive him?
- What does he **want** from this — glory for England, pay, adventure, or is he running from something?
- Has he and [[The Pirate]] **crossed paths before** — did he ever hunt that pirate, or his kind?
- For a man who fakes his faith, how does he handle a mission that is, at its core, **about God and the not-quite-human**?
