# The Outcast Scholar

> **Penuel.** The Nephilim who washed up on Europe's shore in the late 1520s — an outcast scholar who crossed **on purpose**, to seek **Satan** on a world his own people believe Yahweh destroyed, and **died getting here**, on the threshold. His arrival is what sets the whole 1500s story in motion. *(His name is Hebrew for **"face of God"** [Gen 32:30] — a Nephilim who crossed to meet his god face to face, and never saw it. This note records the truth; the mission doesn't have it — see below.)*

## At a glance

- **Role:** the inciting incident — the body and the mystery the Pope's mission is sent to investigate
- **Nature:** Nephilim — very likely a **pure Nephilim** (the giant upper caste; see [[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]), and a fervent **[[Saraïm]] worshipper of the Morning Star**
- **Status:** **dead** (died on arrival)
- **Location:** a shore in Europe, late 1520s

## What the mission knows (vs. the truth)

This note records the **truth** about him. **The mission does not have it.** To the Pope's crew he is only:

- a **body that is plainly not human** — a **giant, 8–10 feet tall**, very likely **deformed** ([[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]) — and
- the author of a **barely-readable diary** about reaching Earth — which, once [[The Lutheran|Konrad]] cracks it, reveals its writer **worshipped the Morning Star and crossed seeking Satan himself.** To the crew, that is nearly as alarming as the body.

They do **not** know he is a "Nephilim," that he was an *outcast*, or what he fled. **"Nephilim" is, at most, a dreaded hypothesis** — the kind of scriptural guess a theologian like [[The Lutheran]] or [[The Chaplain-Knight]] might fear — not a fact they can confirm. *Discovering what he really was is part of the story.*

## What happened

- He **crossed deliberately** ([[The Crossing]]) — not an accident — to **investigate Earth**, though it is **forbidden** ([[Why Earth Is Forbidden]]).
- **What drove him:** a **fervent worshipper of the Morning Star** ([[Saraïm]]'s faith), he noticed the cultures' Earth-stories **conflicted** — his own people insist Yahweh *destroyed* Earth; others merely forbid it. That contradiction convinced him **Earth survived** — and if it survived, perhaps so did **Satan**, the Morning Star who supposedly empowered them. He crossed to **find the Morning Star and speak with him, if he yet lived.**
- That is likely **why he's an outcast:** to long for a journey to a world his own people call *rubble*, in search of a god he hoped to meet face to face, was heresy or madness even among Satan-worshippers.
- He was **more a scholar than a sailor**, traveling in a **tiny boat**. He **crashed and died** making the crossing. *(As a Nephilim he'd have known the real **method** of crossing — but knowing the method isn't the same as being able to **sail**, and he was far better at the former than the latter.)*
- The crew finds his **diary**, written in a language that **clearly descends from ancient Hebrew, but has drifted far from it** — the way modern, slangy English has drifted from Old English. Only someone **trained in the old tongue** (a true Hebrew scholar — see [[The Lutheran]]) can even *attempt* to field-test a reading: he knows the ancestor and must bridge centuries of change to puzzle out the descendant. **Most of it is simply how he meant to reach Earth and why he wanted to come** — little about his world or his kind. From the *how*, scholars **reverse-engineer the crossing** ([[The Crossing]]); from the rest, only a **vague sense** that he came from **somewhere beyond the star.** Enough to turn a dead body into an expedition — but not much more.
- Tellingly, the diary treats crossing as **utterly ordinary** — he mentions it **almost in passing**, as he simply *decides* to use it to reach Earth. Humans had to **reverse-engineer** the method precisely because he never bothered to *explain* it; to him it was as unremarkable as catching a ship to another continent.

## Why he matters

- *(To us, and to the world eventually:)* a Nephilim has not set foot on Earth since the Flood — his body **could be** proof the old stories are real. The mission doesn't know that yet (see above).
- He defied his own people's deepest prohibition to come here — that alone says something about him, and about the cracks in Nephilim doctrine ([[Why Earth Is Forbidden]]).
- **His coming is the hinge of the whole cosmology:** a Nephilim walking into Satan's prison-world is what finally **reveals the Nephilim's existence to the [[The Morning Star|Morning Star]].** He was *right* that Satan lived — and died on the threshold, never reaching him.

## Open questions

- **What else is in the diary?** It's sparse — what did he choose to write down, and what did he leave out?
- The tongue **clearly descends from ancient Hebrew** — what does that shared root say about the Nephilim and their **antediluvian origins**? (They were *here*, in the pre-Flood world, speaking a kindred tongue — a clue to what they *are*.)
- What did he expect to *find* — the Morning Star enthroned, in chains, or long gone? And what would he have done if he'd met him?
- Confirm he's a **[[Saraïm]] outcast** — cast out for believing Earth survived and craving to seek Satan there.
- Did anyone (Nephilim or otherwise) know he was coming?
- Confirm he's a **pure Nephilim** (an 8–10 ft giant) — it fits a learned scholar of the upper caste, and the diary's sophistication points that way.
