# The Lutheran

> **Konrad Schreiber.** A belligerent, self-taught Hebrew prodigy and devout **Luther disciple** — the one man who can field-test the dead Nephilim's diary, and so a **heretic forced to serve the Pope he despises.**

## At a glance

- **Role:** the mission's **translator and scholar** — the reason its knowledge exists at all
- **From:** Germany
- **Age:** **mid-twenties** — a prodigy
- **Faith:** **Lutheran** — a fervent **Luther disciple**; a "heretic" in Catholic eyes
- **Mind:** a **super-genius**; **borderline anti-social**, autistic-coded — hyper-focused, blunt, far happier with texts than with people
- **Temperament:** **belligerent** — as combative as Luther himself
- **Status:** alive
- **Brings:** **self-taught mastery of Hebrew** and ancient languages — quite simply the **best translator alive**

## Personality & drives

- **A super-genius linguistic theologian.** Language and scripture are the two poles of his whole mind, and he is *frighteningly* good at both. **Borderline anti-social and autistic-coded:** he hyper-fixates, misses or ignores social cues, speaks with blunt disregard for feelings, and is far more at ease with a manuscript than a man. To others he is brilliant and exhausting in equal measure; in his own age, people would call him *odd*, *touched*, a strange young genius.
- **A Luther fanboy, through and through.** He reveres the man and the cause, and he's **as belligerent as Luther himself** — combative, contemptuous of authority, quick to quarrel.
- **Feverishly devoted to Hebrew**, which he taught himself **mostly from books**. His burning ambition is to **translate the Old Testament** straight from the source, as his hero did.
- **Genuinely excited about the new worlds.** For all his rage, discovery thrills him.

## Why he's on the mission

- [[The Outcast Scholar|The scholar's diary]] is written in a tongue **descended from ancient Hebrew but heavily evolved** ([[The Crossing]]) — like reading a modern student's slangy diary when all you were trained in is Old English. Only a real Hebrew scholar can even *attempt* it, working from the ancestor-language to bridge the drift.
- He was called because he is, **by far, the best man for it** — not merely trained, but a **genius without equal.** He helped **translate the Bible from Hebrew into the vernacular**, and field-testing a Hebrew-descended tongue is exactly his gift. Other scholars *might* attempt the diary; he is the one who can actually crack it.
- The Catholic Church **won't** do that kind of translation itself: it holds to the **Latin Vulgate** and distrusts vernacular scripture. So the only man who can read the diary is, by necessity, a **Protestant** — and the Pope has to swallow hard and recruit a heretic.
- **Why does he, of all people, accept?** *(Working read — veto if wrong:)* Not for the Pope, whom he loathes. He comes because the diary is the **greatest linguistic find in history** — an unknown tongue descended from ancient Hebrew, and beyond it, **new worlds and living speakers.** His hunger for the language outweighs his disgust at the company. For once, the Church holds something he wants more than he hates them.

## The friction

- **He despises working with Catholics** — and it is **on the orders of the Pope himself**, the very man a Luther disciple holds as the great corruptor of the faith (Luther branded the papacy the Antichrist). Serving a papal mission is, to him, a standing humiliation.
- He's the natural foil to [[The Chaplain-Knight]] — the zealous Catholic leader who would **die for the Pope** the German **loathes**. They **need each other and can't stand each other:** the knight cannot read the diary; the Lutheran cannot command the mission.
- He needles the knight by calling him **"Fra'"** — reducing him to a relic of his **disbanded** order — while the knight insists on **"Don,"** his dignity as a priest. A small, constant war of address.
- His Protestant instinct — go to the **source** (the Hebrew, the text, the evidence) over Church authority — is the very thing that makes him useful, and the very thing that unsettles the others.

## The Nephilim contradiction

He is at war with himself over the Nephilim:

- **He hates them — for not being human.** Things outside God's image, soulless; the revulsion is real and reflexive.
- **And yet he aches to talk to them.** His **linguistic hyper-fixation** overrides his disgust: a living being who speaks a tongue descended from his beloved Hebrew is the most fascinating thing he can imagine. He would sit and converse with the very creatures he despises, just to hear the language live.

The bigot and the obsessive in one skull, neither winning — this is his engine.

## On the Nephilim

- **They all need to die** — his sole point of agreement with [[The Chaplain-Knight|the Catholic zealot]] (see [[Real-World Nephilim Theology]]).
- Yet his **linguistic hunger wars with it** — he aches to *talk* to one before it dies (see the Nephilim contradiction above).
- Like the knight, he's **decently abolitionist** on slavery — a value the Nephilim caste order ([[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]) will test.
- As a Hebraist, he's the one most able to recognize the old **fallen-angel/Watchers** reading once the body demands it.

## Open questions

- How does he square what the diary implies (other peoples; soulless beings; a Hebrew-kin tongue older than the Church) with his **sola-scriptura** theology? A text that isn't scripture yet predates the Church must rattle him.
- Does his **fascination ever beat his hatred** — could he come to respect, even like, a Nephilim? (A natural arc.)
- How far does his belligerence go on a tiny boat where the **leader is a Catholic he must obey**?
- Has the Reformation cost him anything **personal** — persecution, exile, lost family?
- His fellow **Lutherans just sacked Rome (1527)** and humiliated [[Pope Clement VII]] — does he gloat, feel implicated, or both, now that he serves that very pope?
