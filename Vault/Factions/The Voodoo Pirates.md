# The Voodoo Pirates

> Sincere believers, tricked into sin — and the only humans known to have crossed to another world and come back.

## At a glance

- **Type:** loose pirate group / culture
- **Defining trait:** they've **crossed and returned**, which almost no one else has
- **Reach:** TBD

## What sets them apart

- They are **the only known humans to survive a crossing and make it back** ([[The Crossing]]).
- The truth, though, is that their return was **luck, not power** — they happened to turn the right way and live. They have no reliable method, whatever they believe.
- A **real method does exist** — but it belongs to the **Nephilim**, not them ([[The Nephilim Worlds]]). No human has it. Their "voodoo" is not it.

## Their "voodoo"

- They **genuinely believe** in their practices.
- But per [[Setting Principles]], **nothing supernatural actually happens.** The rituals do nothing.
- What's really going on is **spiritual corruption** — they're being **tricked into deeper sin** under the impression they're working magic.

## A cautionary tale: the doomed star-chase

A telling example of the voodoo pirates in action — and of how their beliefs **damn rather than empower** them:

- One crew kept an **astrology witch** who **half-knew of the star and the crossing from old legends** ([[The Crossing]]).
- She **overhyped** it, promising the star led to **riches**, and the crew chased it on her word.
- They crossed — and found not treasure but **sea monsters.** They fled; **most were eaten**, and only a few survived, by luck.
- [[The Pirate]] was one of those few. Her "magic" gave them nothing; her false promise simply led good men into the dark to die.

This is the pattern: real fragments of truth, wrapped in lies, leading believers to **ruin** instead of power.

## Connections

- [[The Pirate]] — one of their survivors; not deeply into the voodoo, and willing to work with the Church.
- [[The Crossing]] — the journey they're known for surviving.
