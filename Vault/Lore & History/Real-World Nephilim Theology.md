# Real-World Nephilim Theology

> Reference: the actual biblical texts and the main historical interpretations of the Nephilim — and who held them. Grounds the setting's theology. *(Real-world scholarship, not in-world canon.)*

## The biblical basis

- **Genesis 6:1–4** — the "**sons of God**" (*bənê hāʼĕlōhîm*) take the "daughters of men" as wives; "the **Nephilim** were on the earth in those days, and also afterward… the mighty men of old, men of renown." Immediately precedes the Flood.
- **Numbers 13:33** — the spies in Canaan: "we saw the Nephilim (the sons of Anak)… we were like grasshoppers." → giants, *after* the Flood.
- **Deuteronomy 2–3** — giant clans: Rephaim, Anakim, Emim, Zamzummim (and Og of Bashan).
- **Jude 6 / 2 Peter 2:4** — angels who "did not keep their proper domain," chained in darkness — read by many as the Watchers.
- **Septuagint** renders *Nephilim* as **gigantes**, "giants."

## Who were the "sons of God"? — the three main theories

**1. Fallen Angels / "Watchers" (the supernatural-hybrid view)**
- Angels lusted after women, mated with them, and fathered the Nephilim — **giant hybrid offspring.**
- Elaborated in **1 Enoch (Book of the Watchers)**: ~200 Watchers (led by Shemihazah/Azazel) descend on Mt. Hermon, take wives, teach forbidden arts (weapons, sorcery, cosmetics), father violent giants; the giants' **spirits become demons** when they die. Also **Jubilees**, the Genesis Apocryphon.
- **Holders:** dominant in **Second Temple Judaism**; many **early Church Fathers** (Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Athenagoras, Clement of Alexandria, Lactantius); echoed in **Jude / 2 Peter.**
- The **oldest** view — and the one the setting treats as **true** ([[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]).

**2. Sethite / godly-line (the human view)**
- "Sons of God" = the **godly line of Seth**; "daughters of men" = the **ungodly line of Cain.** Their intermarriage corrupts humanity. Nephilim = merely **mighty, violent men**, nothing supernatural.
- Rose to dominance with **Augustine** (*City of God*) and **Chrysostom**; the **mainstream Western view** through the Middle Ages.
- **Holders in the setting's era (1520s–40s):** the orthodox default — **Augustine's legacy**, **Aquinas** (angels cannot truly procreate), and the Reformers **Luther and Calvin** both held versions. → what the crew's churchmen would *default* to.

**3. Dynastic rulers / "sons of judges" (the kingly view)**
- "Sons of God" = powerful **human rulers / divine-kings / nobles**; Nephilim = the violent offspring of tyrants.
- **Holders:** much **rabbinic / Targumic** tradition (Targum Onkelos: "sons of nobles/judges"; Shimon bar Yochai reportedly cursed those who said "sons of God"). Also a favorite of later/modern scholarship.

## Other strands

- **Etymology:** *Nephilim* from *nāphal*, "to fall" → "the fallen ones"; or Aramaic *nephîlā*, "giant." LXX: gigantes.
- **Demonology:** in the Enochic stream, the **disembodied spirits of dead giants = demons** — foundational to Second Temple demonology.
- **The Flood problem:** Genesis says Nephilim were around "and also afterward," yet the Flood should have wiped them out (Num. 13 finds giants later). Explanations: a **second angelic incursion** after the Flood; survival through Noah's line; or the Sethite reading, which sidesteps it.

## Why this matters for the setting (the irony)

- **The orthodox view of the 1520s–40s is the Sethite/human one** — so the crew's theologians ([[The Chaplain-Knight]], even [[The Lutheran]]) would *default to "corrupt men, not monsters."* In your canon, **that mainstream view is wrong.**
- The **fallen-angel / Watchers view — ancient, by the 1500s sidelined as apocryphal — is the *true* one.** And **1 Enoch was essentially unknown in Western Europe** until it was brought back from Ethiopia in the 1770s — so almost no one in the crew's world holds the text that explains what they're dealing with.
- **The Lutheran**, a Hebraist who reads rabbinic material, is the one most likely to know the old angelic and "sons of judges" readings — making him, again, the key to the truth.
- **The arc (canon):** the world holds the **Sethite view** as orthodoxy — until [[The Outcast Scholar|the giant corpse]], and what follows, **forces the fallen-angel / Watchers view to be true.** The story is, in part, the death of the comfortable reading.

## The crew's positions (on the Nephilim)

- **[[The Chaplain-Knight]] (Catholic zealot):** **they all need to die**, eventually. His one true point of agreement with the Lutheran.
- **[[The Lutheran]] (belligerent reformer):** likewise — **they all need to die** — *the only thing he and the knight agree on.* (His linguistic hunger still wars with it: he aches to *talk* to one first.) Both men are also **decently abolitionist** on slavery, which may bite when they meet the Nephilim's fear-bound underclass.
- **[[The Captain]] (worldly Englishman):** **doesn't much care if they live or die.** He sees them as he sees **the "savages" his motherland rules** — lessers and subjects, a matter of empire, not theology.
- **[[The Pirate]] (streetwise survivor):** no patience for the **"hooty-tooty theology"** — to him they're **just mythological creatures**, monsters, nothing more. And unlike the others, he's actually *seen* them.
