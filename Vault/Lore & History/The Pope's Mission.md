# The Pope's Mission

> The Vatican expedition at the heart of the 1500s storyline (**late 1520s**): a hand-picked crew sent to investigate a **dead, not-quite-human body** — who end up stranded on an alien world.

## Origin

A dead body that is **plainly not human** washes up on the shores of Europe ([[The Outcast Scholar]] — though the mission has **no idea what it really is**). With it: a **diary in a language close to ancient Hebrew.**

Scholars manage to read it, but it yields **less than they'd hope.** Most of it is simply **how its writer meant to reach Earth** and **why he wanted to come.** From the *how*, they **reverse-engineer the crossing** ([[The Crossing]]); from the rest, only a **vague sense** that the thing came from **somewhere beyond the star.** They are **not even sure it is a "Nephilim."**

So what they actually hold is modest: a **non-human corpse**, a **dawning awareness that *something* is out there**, and a **stolen, half-finished way to go and look.**

## What the Pope wants — and why it's him

*The pope is [[Pope Clement VII]] (r. 1523–1534), freshly humiliated by the **1527 Sack of Rome** — battered, but still the figurehead of Christendom.*

- **He wants an investigation. That's all.** Not conquest, not a relic, not a pre-emptive strike — just to learn **what this is, where it came from, and what it means.**
- **Why a non-human body is the Pope's business:** such a thing is **inescapably theological** — it stirs up creation, souls, scripture, the order of God's world. That falls to the Church by right.
- **Why he can command it:** in this era the Pope is the **nearest thing to a figurehead over all of Europe** — the one authority who can convene a **cross-national crew** (Catholic, Protestant, even a pirate) under a single banner. Tellingly, the only man who can read the diary is a **Lutheran** ([[The Lutheran]]) — so the Pope must seat a **heretic at the heart of his holy work.**

## The crew (principals)

- [[The Chaplain-Knight]] — **leader**; Italian survivor of Rhodes, zealot, the Pope's most loyal man.
- [[The Pirate]] — one of the only humans to survive a [[The Crossing|crossing]] and return; their guide to the other side.
- [[The Lutheran]] — genius German Hebrew scholar; the **only one who can read the diary**, and a heretic at the heart of a papal mission.
- [[The Captain]] — renowned English sea-captain; commands the ship, and brings the **best crew afloat** (which is the whole point of him).

## How the voyage is crewed (and why)

The mission sails on a **proper ship with a full, ordinary crew.** *(In truth a small boat would have done — the crossing only needs you **out of sight of land**, not an ocean away — but they **didn't know that.** Reverse-engineering the diary, they assumed a long blue-water voyage and prepared for one. A reasonable mistake — see [[The Crossing#You don't need a big ship]].)*

- The four principals are the **away team**; the **small boat** is for landing and investigation.
- The **monsters thin the big crew to four** regardless of how they'd traveled.

## The turning point (the "stranding" beat)

The crew is convinced to sail at the star — through the **Pope's command** and faith in the **reverse-engineered method** ([[The Crossing]]). The star itself doesn't kill them. **The far side does:**

1. They **cross** — full ship, full crew.
2. In the destination world's waters, **sea monsters attack and kill most of the crew** — the same horror [[The Pirate]] barely survived once before, the alien life of [[The Known Worlds|other seas]].
3. The **few survivors — the principals among them — are stranded** on the alien world: too few to sail, the ship lost or crippled, and the **way home unsolved** anyway ([[The Crossing]]).

The survivors carry it with them: they lived while most of the crew were devoured. **Survivor's guilt** hangs over the whole marooned arc — see the tone below.

## The shape of the story

> **Marooned → learn what they can → (maybe do one bold thing) → find a way home.**
> **Tone: crash-landed behind enemy lines — shot through with survivor's guilt.**

Stranded on one of [[The Known Worlds]], the survivors must **stay alive, gather every scrap of intelligence** about whatever it was that washed ashore and the worlds it came from, and somehow **get home** — carrying what they've learned back with them.

While there, they **discover the real stakes**: the [[The Morning Star|Morning Star]] — Satan — is **alive**, caged on Earth by God, and **the Nephilim must never find out.** Should the [[Saraïm]] learn he lives, he will drive them to **invade Earth** ([[The Morning Star]]). What began as reconnaissance becomes a desperate effort to **protect that secret** and carry it home. The arc plays like a **crash-landed-behind-enemy-lines** story: survival, recon, **a secret to keep**, and exfiltration — all underneath **the weight of the dead**, most of the crew eaten so these few could wash ashore. *(This is the seed of the later **invasion** storyline: the day the Saraïm find out.)*
