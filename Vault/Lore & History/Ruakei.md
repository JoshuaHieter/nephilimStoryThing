# Ruakei — the Eelfolk

> The despised, goblinish eel-hybrids of the Nephilim worlds — the "unclean" bottom caste of [[Saraïm]], and the besieged free people of the [[Book Plotline|book's]] opening. Slippery survivors who go where no one else can.

## Names

- **Ruakei**: what the eelfolk **call themselves** (Māori-shaped, see Language below).
- **Shiqtzim**, "the Abominations": what the **[[Saraïm]] call them**. Leviticus 11:10 calls water creatures without fins and scales *sheqetz*, "an abomination to you." It's a slur with scripture behind it.
- *(Formerly "Tunoth"; renamed because it was too close to [[Toldoth]].)*

## Body

- **Stock:** family **Anguillidae** (eels) — an alien genus ([[Kilayim Races]]).
- **Goblinish.** **Skinny humanoids of human height** (short beside an 8–10 ft Tahor), with **black skin**, big freaky **eel eyes**, and a wide, toothy **eel mouth.** Wet, dark, unsettling to look at.
- **The shape:** picture a person with the head taken off and an **eel's head set where the human head was**. The eel's body runs **all the way down the back**, making a **big eel bulge along the spine**, and comes out as a **tail** of decent length. The human parts are covered in **eel skin**.
- **Capped at half-animal** (a Saraïm underclass race) — humanoid in frame, eel in head, spine-ridge, tail and skin.
- **Low IQ** — cunning in a scrappy, gutter way, but no scholars.

## Biology

- **Slime-slick & sinuous.** A protective slime coat means **you can't get a grip on one** — natural escape artists, net-slippers, hard to hold or pen.
- **Amphibious.** They breathe air but must **stay moist**, and can **hold their breath and work underwater for ages** — which is *why* they do the **dock, dive, and sea labor** no one else can, and why they alone dare the **monster-haunted shallows.**
- **Extraordinary smell.** Like real eels, they have one of the keenest noses of any creature: they can **smell blood, fear, or a person through water and mud.**
- **Sex set by crowding.** As in real eels, **crowded young turn male, sparse young turn female**. Ruakei born in cramped Saraïm slums come out **mostly male**; free [[Pātuna]] raises **more daughters**. Bondage literally reshapes the people.
- **Pale children.** Infants are **pale and a little red**, because you can see their insides through the skin (like real "glass eels"). They darken to black as they grow.
- **Silvering.** In old age a Ruakei **silvers**, like a real eel before its last migration: the skin turns silver, the eyes grow huge, and they feel a **pull toward the open sea**. Answering it is a **sacred last walk into the sea**: *the water takes back its own.*
- **Wet, clicky speech.** Through an eel's mouth, their speech is **wet and clicky**, at least to Saraïm ears.

## Origin (why eelfolk exist)

Like every [[Kilayim Races|Kilayim]], the Ruakei are **Nephilim crossed with a native stock** — here, a **native amphibious eel-creature** of Rephaim's marshes and shallows (family **Anguillidae**, alien genus). Scaleless, water-bound, and lowly, they were slotted into [[Saraïm]]'s **bottom "unclean" caste** from the very start.

## Folklore — the Great Eel & the Trickster

The eelfolk's origin myth — *their* truth, fiercely held:

- **The Mother — the Great Eel, *Whaetunu*.** A **queen of the eels** and a **goddess who birthed the marsh** itself. In those days, they say, **eels were a sentient people**, her subjects.
- **The Father — a trickster Nephilim, *Māwiel*.** A cunning wanderer who came to the marsh to **catch the Great Eel for food** — but she's a trickster too, slipping every net and **bantering back**, and their contest of wits turned to **love.** Two tricksters who bonded over the chase. Their children became the **eelfolk:** her water, *both* their cunning.
- **The Curse.** When the eelfolk were born, the **[[Saraïm]] cursed the eels to lose their speech** — which is why the **wild eels of the marsh seem dumb beasts now**, no longer the speaking people they were. A stolen birthright; a fallen kin.
- **Cousins, not beasts.** So the eelfolk treat the **wild eels as kin — cousins who are *still people*, simply robbed of speech.** They'd no sooner eat one than eat a relative; they speak *to* them, tend them, mourn them. *(Deeper than the [[Toldoth]]'s love of the wild ayal, which is fondness for a beloved animal, not a person.)*

The myth gives them **pride against contempt:** they descend from **a goddess and a trickster clever enough to win her**, not groveling slave-stock. And it carries their **freedom-creed** — *every net has a hole; the water always takes back its own* — which is literally [[Pātuna]]'s founding spirit.

> **The truth they refuse:** there was no queen, no goddess, no curse. It was just **horny Nephilim** breeding with the native eel-beasts (Nephilim crossbreed with anything — [[Nephilim Biology & Caste]]); the wild eels were never sentient, only animals. The eelfolk **don't want to believe that** — and who could blame them. *(Low magic: the "curse" is myth, not real — [[Setting Principles]].)*

### Their names
- ***Whaetunu*** (the Great Eel): a near-miss on Māori *whaea* "mother" + *tuna* "eel." Pure marsh-shaped.
- ***Māwiel*** (the Trickster): a near-miss on **Māui**, the Polynesian trickster, plus the Hebrew ***-el***. Since he is a Nephilim, his name carries a Hebrew, angelic edge (the language's Hebrew gradient).
- *(Real-myth echo: in Polynesian legend the trickster Māui **kills** Tuna, an eel lover. The Ruakei story inverts it: the trickster **wins** the eel through love.)*

## Wider religion: heroes made demigods

- Beyond the Whaetunu myth, Ruakei faith is a host of **famous heroes** whose stories have been **aggrandized into demigods**.
- Each hero's legend **explains a natural phenomenon or a piece of history**.

## Place in the world

- **Constellation:** the eelfolk's sanctified caste role belongs to **Baraqiel** (Storm & Sea) in [[The Saraïm Faith]].

- **The "unclean" caste.** In the Hebrew-derived worldview of [[Saraïm]], a **scaleless thing is ritually unclean** — so the eelfolk are the **despised bottom caste**, doing the unclean wet-work: bilges, sewers, corpse-hauling, diving, the harbor's filth. Their oppression carries a **religious** justification, which makes it bite all the harder.
- **Free, but weak.** Some live as their own **[[The Ocean World|Iyim]] city-states** — sovereign but fragile. One such — **[[Pātuna]]** — is under [[Saraïm]] siege in the [[Book Plotline]].

## Culture (Māori-leaning)

- An **eel-people of weir and tide** — communal, insular, water-bound; eels and the sea are kin and ancestor. To them the land-castes are the poor cousins; **the water is theirs.**

## Language

- **Mostly Māori-inspired dialect**: it borrows the **broad strokes of how Māori words are formed**, **not the actual words**.
  - *(Reference, Māori sound shape: open syllables, each a consonant plus vowel or a bare vowel; no consonant clusters; words end in vowels; a small sound set: a e i o u, h k m n p r t w, ng, wh; long vowels; reduplication.)*
- **Exception:** **[[Pātuna]]** keeps its name, even though it is built from real Māori words (*pā tuna*).
- **The Hebrew gradient:** the **more Hebrew a word is, the more advanced it is, or the more it has to do with [[Saraïm]]**. Old, home, water and kin words keep the Māori shape. Technology, trade, law and the empire's things come in Hebrew-shaped.

## Character as a people

- **Slippery survivors.** They slip nets, oppression, and notice alike — go-anywhere folk (through bilges, under hulls, flooded tunnels), with a name as **smugglers.** Weak in open war, but **impossible to truly stamp out.**

## When cornered

- They fight **slippery and tricky**: slipping grips and nets, ambush, misdirection and escape, rather than standing in open battle.
