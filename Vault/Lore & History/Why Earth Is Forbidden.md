# Why Earth Is Forbidden

> Among the Nephilim, going to Earth is forbidden. **No one alive knows the real reason, or that Earth survived.** Every culture wraps the rule in its own story, and only [[The Outcast Scholar|Penuel]] suspected the truth. He was branded a heretic for it.

## The true reason (lost)

The original justification, now **forgotten by every Nephilim culture**. Nobody on the Nephilim worlds believes Earth still exists:

- **Humans have divine favor.** The Nephilim know it.
- **The Nephilim, by contrast, have no souls** — or none that can be **redeemed** — and they know it. (A strong but fallen **angelic will**, with no salvation on offer; see [[Nephilim Biology & Caste]].)
- They fear that **if humans ever discovered them, humans would be ordered to exterminate them** — exactly as happened at the **Flood** ([[Nephilim & the Flood]]).

So the prohibition is, at root, **self-preservation born of guilt and fear.** Stay away from Earth, stay unnoticed, stay alive.

## How the cultures disguise it

The real reason is lost. The various [[The Nephilim Worlds|Nephilim cultures]] each cloak the rule in their own way, for example:

- *"You shouldn't go to Earth."* (a taboo, no reason given)
- *"Earth doesn't exist."* (denial — the place is a myth, a lie, nothing)
- *"Yahweh destroyed Earth in the Flood."* (the **[[Saraïm]]** line — Earth is the enemy's rubble, nothing there but ruin; said in triumph, not fear)
- *"Earth is a fairytale."* (the **[[Toldoth]]** line: a story told to subject the constellations and turn the inner paths into indulgences)
- *(no story at all)* (the **[[Gibborim]]** don't even know Earth exists)
- *(other framings — TBD: the isles…)*

**Crucially, these cover-stories *conflict* with one another** — and that contradiction is itself a crack in the wall. It's what made [[The Outcast Scholar]] suspicious enough to investigate: if every culture forbids Earth but for different, incompatible reasons, *something* real is being hidden behind them all. Each power braids its **religion, culture, and fear of Earth** into a single story — and braids it *differently* (the [[Saraïm]]'s is Morning-Star conquest; [[Toldoth]]'s and [[Gibborim]]'s remain to be set).

## Why it matters

- [[The Outcast Scholar]] **broke this prohibition** to study Earth — and died doing it. Whatever he believed about Earth, he believed it strongly enough to defy the deepest rule his people have.
- **The prohibition's nightmare is now unfolding — because of him.** His diary is handing humans the two things the Nephilim most feared they'd learn: that the Nephilim **exist out there**, and **how to cross** ([[The Crossing]], [[The Pope's Mission]]). The forbidden world is coming — armed with a dead Nephilim's own words.
