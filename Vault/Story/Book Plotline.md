# Book Plotline

> *Of Giants and Grasshoppers*, Book 1 — **title = its year** (late 1520s, exact year TBD).
>
> The **strict, set** narrative — the novel. The four crash into a besieged **[[Tunoth|eelfolk]]** [[The Ocean World|Iyim]] city, **save it — and are betrayed the moment it's safe.** A deadly secret must be **buried in blood**, and they fight their way **home** — two of them dying so the other two can carry the truth back to Earth. Behind enemy lines the whole way.

## Continuity

- Shared setup: [[The Pope's Mission]] → [[The Crossing]] → sea monsters take most of the crew → the four survivors wash ashore.
- This is the **fixed** version; events here are canon **for the book.**

## Form, length & series model

- **Series model:** a **shared-world anthology** — each book is a **mostly self-contained story** in the same world, with **some overlapping characters.** Not one continuous serial.
- **Form:** **third-person rotating-limited**, prose **voice-colored to each POV** — and every third-person chapter **opens with an in-world personal document:** [[The Lutheran|Konrad]]'s letters to a friend in Wittenberg, [[The Captain|Drake]]'s ship's log, [[The Chaplain-Knight|Bartolomeo]]'s confessions/prayers, and fragments of [[The Outcast Scholar|the scholar's translated diary]] (the reader's dramatic-irony window onto the [[The Morning Star|Satan-secret]]).
- **Length target:** *a little shorter than* Mistborn: *The Final Empire* — roughly **150–200k words**, a substantial self-contained epic.
- **POV plan & arc-questions:** Konrad (primary lens — *can fascination beat hate?*); Bartolomeo (moral/authority — *what survives when the comfortable doctrine dies?*); Drake (grounded/command — *does the guilt break or harden him?*); Mateus (the heart — *will he reach for mercy again after it's betrayed?*). Match POV to scene; one POV per scene.

## The landing (opening)

- The four wash up at **[[Pātuna]]** — a weak, free [[Tunoth|eelfolk]] [[The Ocean World|Iyim]] city-state on a **marshy island**, a fortress of runaways on [[Saraïm]]'s border — **already under siege.** The marsh makes it nearly impossible to storm, and they fish for endless food, so the siege is a **grinding stalemate.**
- They crash straight into a **war zone**: an oppressed free people on the verge of conquest by the Satan-worshipping empire.
- First contact is with the **besieged eelfolk**; [[The Lutheran|Konrad]]'s translation is the crew's lifeline from the first hour.

## Why this opening works (for a book)

- A **fixed, legible situation** (a siege) with immediate stakes — good for a tight authored plot.
- Drops the crew among **sympathetic underdogs** (the free eelfolk) against the **main antagonist faction** ([[Saraïm]]) at once.
- The crew's **gunpowder** could matter against a besieging army that has **no guns** ([[The Ocean World]]).

## Outline — tentpole method

*All tentpoles and eighths placed. Whole book: **behind enemy lines, no safe ground.** Length ~150–200k; siege ≈ the first third.*

- **START (0%):** scholar prologue → 1520s Europe: the giant corpse, the near-Hebrew diary, the Pope assembling the mixed crew.
- **~9% — DEPARTURE *(brief):*** the team clicks shut — **Mateus** the last piece, dragged aboard **off the gallows** after his crew's failed raid on the ship — and they sail. Crew frictions go live at once (Konrad's "Fra'" jabs, Drake watching the pirate, Bartolomeo pulling rank); the last of Earth drops behind. *(Little time on the boat.)*
- **~12% — THE CROSSING & THE SHALLOWS-SLAUGHTER:** far out, the star appears; they sail at it; the uncanny crossing ([[The Crossing]]) — sea unchanged, the sky swapping over. Then in the new world's waters the **sea monsters** hit, and **most of the crew are devoured** ([[The Pope's Mission]]). The **four** survive, cast toward an unknown shore — the trauma that founds the survivor's guilt, the crew cut to four and stranded.
- **~25% — LOCK-IN:** in the marsh city the crew **throw in with the [[Tunoth|eelfolk]].** Their **gunpowder** (finite, salvaged — see [[The Ocean World]]) is a weapon these besieged goblins have never seen; it makes the crew valued defenders and buys a taste of **hope** (though it can't break the stalemate alone). Here **Mateus befriends one eelfolk in particular** — and **Konrad lays down the rule: guard the truth of Earth and where they came from.** Point of no return into the city's war.
- **~33% — THE CONFIDED SECRET, AND THE BETRAYAL:** worn down and aching to unburden his past, **Mateus confides the secret of Earth to his eelfolk friend** — breaking Konrad's rule, trusting the friendship. The friend **swears to keep it** — then **trades it as a bargaining chip to save the city:** the intelligence that *Earth survived, and humans are here* is explosive enough to **buy the siege's end.** **The city is saved — by selling out the crew.** The friend's treachery (and Mateus's own slip) **shatters his hope in the Kilayim** and leaves him **complicit in the catastrophe.** *(Act 1 = the siege + the tour of the world; [[Gibborim]] and pure [[Nephilim Biology & Caste|Tahorim]] stay mostly offscreen.)*
- **~35% — THE FRIEND'S HEAD (right after the siege lifts):** Mateus, **heartbroken** the secret is out, confronts his friend — who is **baffled it's even a problem** (his whole city is *saved* — what's the harm?). The crew get two sentences out of him: *who else did you tell?* **No one** — only the buyer. Then **Bartolomeo lops his head off**, cold and certain (a soulless Kilayim, a loose end — no sin in his eyes), **horrifying Mateus.** First blood — and the leak now sits with one man: the *sar*.
- **THE NEW PROBLEM (the manhunt):** the secret is loose — the [[Saraïm]] **siege-commander** (a *sar*) bought it, and means to carry it **personally up the chain** for the **glory** of being the one to deliver it. That ambition is the crew's salvation: because **everyone who learns it hoards it for their own advancement** — telling *only* their higher-up — the secret travels a **thin vertical line**, not a wildfire. So the crew don't face a genocide; they chase a **short chain of glory-hungry men**, racing the report up toward the capital. Kill the holders before it lands. A hunt with a ticking clock (→ [[The Morning Star|Satan, invasion]]).
- **~38% — HUNTED:** betrayed and exposed, the crew flee the very city they saved — **no safe ground anywhere**, the [[Saraïm]] now aware that humans walk Rephaim. Fugitives, they go to ground and begin **hunting the one who bought the secret.** Mateus is hollowed out — and **guilty.** *(Sets up the 50% turn.)*
- **MIDPOINT (~50%): the full horror lands — and it makes them killers.** Konrad, working the diary with new [[Saraïm]] context, grasps the real stakes: the Morning Star is **no myth — [[The Morning Star|Satan is alive]]**, and a leak means **invasion / apocalypse.** It forces the unthinkable — they stop merely *fleeing* and **commit to murder** to seal the secret. **Survivors → assassins**, the weight falling hardest on the two faithful men. *(Along the way the disillusioned Mateus is brought to **Protestant faith** by Konrad — giving his coming martyrdom its meaning. And throughout, Konrad is cracking the **full** method — including the way **home** — see [[The Crossing]].)*
- **~62% — THE CHASE TIGHTENS, AND MATEUS FINDS FAITH:** the crew pursue the *sar* across enemy country toward the capital — a grim, costly run behind enemy lines (powder spent, a wound, a near-capture). They **fail to catch him in the open** and grasp the terrible truth: to stop him they must **follow him into the capital, to the throne itself** — all but suicidal. In that dark resolve, at a low ebb, **Konrad finally reaches Mateus** — the hollowed, guilt-ridden pirate is **brought to Protestant faith**, given something to die for just before the end. *(The conversion that makes his coming martyrdom mean something — and the first crack in Konrad's own hardness.)*
- **~75% — CRISIS (the kill):** they chase the *sar* to the capital, **[[Zebul]]**, but arrive **a beat too late** — he's already reached the **[[Saraïm#The throne (top of the chain)|Melek]]** (and, hoarding the glory, told *no one else*). So they must kill **both** — the *sar* and the inbred god-king — to seal it. **The kill succeeds, but Mateus dies completing it**, and now they're **regicides, hunted**, fleeing a roused empire. Victory at the cost of their best man — and at a terrible new price (the Seed, below).
- **~82% — CORNERED, AND THE TRUTH THAT BREAKS KONRAD:** the flight to the sea is overtaken near the coast — escape almost in reach, the enemy closing. Here **Konrad grasps the regicide buried nothing:** the [[The Saraïm Priesthood|priests]] will hunt their king's killers, and that hunt will find Earth regardless. Getting home isn't escape — it's racing to **deliver a warning.** *His war-hawk resolve is born: the West must arm and strike first.*
- **~88% — BARTOLOMEO'S LAST STAND:** cornered with the sea at their backs, **Bartolomeo holds the pursuers** so the other two can reach a boat — and **dies.** Edmund, watching the priest he mocked die for him, **finally takes his faith seriously.**
- **END (~95–100%): home.** Just two left — and **fittingly the only two who *can* get home:** [[The Lutheran|Konrad]], who **cracked the full method** (which star is Earth's, how to steer home — [[The Crossing]]), and [[The Captain|Drake]], whose **seamanship** can run a **small boat** through the monster-seas (you never needed a ship — [[The Crossing#You don't need a big ship]]). They sail out, aim for Earth's star, and **cross home** — carrying the warning, the grief, and the dead.
- **THE SEED (the tragic irony):** they buried the *specific* secret — the only two who knew *Satan lives, Earth survived* (the *sar* and the emperor) are dead. **But the regicide itself is the fuse:** [[The Saraïm Priesthood|the priests]] — the empire's real power — are left with a god-king murdered by **mysterious little people who vanished.** *Who were they? Why kill the king? Where did they go?* That question will, in time, **turn the Saraïm toward Earth** — the very invasion the crew tried to prevent, born from the act that "won." *(Series engine.)*

## Character arcs (this book)

*(Book-specific — kept here, not in the shared character profiles, since the [[TTRPG Plotline]] may differ.)*

- **[[The Pirate|Mateus]] — gutter skeptic → martyr.** Thinks the Nephilim are just monsters; **befriends an eelfolk** and, worn down, **confides the secret of Earth** — and the friend **trades it to save the city**, betraying him and **making him complicit in the leak.** Then he watches **Bartolomeo behead the bewildered friend** before his eyes. His hope in the Kilayim shattered, he goes **disillusioned**; Konrad wins him to **Protestant faith**; he **dies completing the mission** — the kill that cleans up his own mistake.
- **[[The Lutheran|Konrad]] — zealot reformer → war-hawk.** **Softens on Catholicism** (through shared suffering and Bartolomeo), and is the one who **brings Mateus to the faith.** Survives — and goes home **set on convincing the West to make war on the other world.** *(Seeds the larger invasion arc.)*
- **[[The Chaplain-Knight|Bartolomeo]] — the static rock.** His arc happened **years ago** (Rhodes); he doesn't change. **His cold certainty shows early — he takes the betrayer-friend's head without a flicker** (the Kilayim are soulless enemies of God). He **mentors Edmund's faith**, then **dies to save them all** — his martyrdom is the hinge that converts Edmund.
- **[[The Captain|Edmund]] — complacent → serious.** Carries **survivor's guilt** for his eaten crew; **annoyed at Bartolomeo's piety**, sure he's *already* a good-enough Catholic. **Bartolomeo's sacrificial death finally makes him take his faith seriously.** Survives, changed.

**The pattern:** two martyrs — **Mateus** (newly converted) and **Bartolomeo** (the lifelong rock) — give their lives to bring the other two home; and the survivors, **Konrad and Edmund**, each leave with their faith remade. A book about four men and belief under an alien sky.
